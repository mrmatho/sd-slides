# Vulnerabilities and Risks

::content::

From the Study Design:

> Types of vulnerabilities and risks within insecure development environments, including:

A **vulnerability** is a weakness in a system that can be exploited by a threat (intentional, accidental or event-based) to cause harm.

A **risk** is the potential for loss or damage when a threat exploits a vulnerability.

We have a list of vulnerabiilities and risks to explore today. For each we need to identify:

- What the vulnerability or risk is
- How it can be exploited
- Why it applies to the software development process

# Use of APIs

::content::

An **API (Application Programming Interface)** is a connection point (through a set of rules and protocols) that allow software applications to communicate with each other. They can be used to access other systems as well as sometimes being used to separate the front-end (user-interface) and back-end of a system.

APIs connect systems, but weakly secured or badly managed ones can leak data, grant unauthorised access, and widen the attack surface.

# Malware

::content::

Malware is malicious software built to disrupt systems, steal data, or gain unauthorised access.

Malware is a concern for the development process because it can be introduced into the development environment, or even into the software being developed (through code libraries).

# Unpatched Software

::content::

Patching and updating software is a critical part of maintaining security. Unpatched software still carries known flaws, leaving systems exposed to attackers who know about existing vulnerabilities.

# Poor Identity and Access Management

::content::

**Identity Management** is the process of maintaining and managing user identities and their access to systems. **Access Management** is the process of controlling who has access to what resources.

Bad identity and access management lets the wrong people in, or keeps the right people out.

# Man-in-the-Middle Attacks

::content::

A **man-in-the-middle attack** intercepts or alters communication between two parties without their knowledge.

Man-in-the-middle attacks are a concern for the development process because they can be inserted into a data pipeline to intercept sensitive data, or to inject malicious code into the software being developed.

# Insider Threats

::content::

Insider threats come from people inside the organisation who misuse access, data, or systems, whether maliciously or carelessly.

Insider threats in a development process include:

- Developers who introduce malicious code into the software being developed
- Developers who accidentally introduce vulnerabilities
- Developers who leak sensitive data from the development environment

# Cyber Security Incidents

::content::

Cyber security incidents are breaches, attacks, or failures that compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of systems and data.

Cyber security incidents in a development process could compromise (among other things):

- The development environment
- The software being developed
- Personal or sensitive data used in the development process

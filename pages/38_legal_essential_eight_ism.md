# Legal: Essential Eight and ISM

::content::

Not all guidance is a law. Some is **industry best practice**, published by the government.

Two important frameworks are:

- The **Essential Eight**
- The **Information Security Manual (ISM)**

Both are published by the **Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC)**.

# What is the Essential Eight?

::content::

The **Essential Eight** is a set of **eight strategies**.

They are a **baseline** - the minimum organisations should do to protect against cyber threats.

They aim to:

- Prevent attacks
- Limit the impact of an attack
- Help recovery if an attack succeeds

::left::

# Essential 8: Prevent Attacks

::left::

### Application control

Only let approved applications run. This stops unknown or malicious programs from executing.

### Patch applications

Fix known vulnerabilities in software quickly, so attackers can't exploit them.

::right::

### Configure Office macro settings

Block or restrict macros in Office files. Macros are little code snippets embedded in Word, PowerPoint, etc. Blocking (or restricting) these closes off a common way malware is delivered.

### User application hardening

Disable features attackers commonly exploit, such as Flash or ads. This reduces the ways a device can be attacked.

# Essential 8: Limit Impact and Recover

::left::

### Restrict administrative privileges

Only give admin access to people who need it. This limits the damage if an account is compromised.

### Patch operating systems

Fix known vulnerabilities in the operating system quickly, so attackers can't exploit them.

::right::

### Multi-factor authentication

Require a second proof of identity to log in. This stops a stolen password alone from granting access.

### Regular backups

Keep secure, tested copies of important data. This allows recovery without paying a ransom or losing data.

# Essential Eight Maturity Levels

::content::

Organisations don't just "pass" or "fail" the Essential Eight.

They are rated on a **maturity level**, from Level 0 to Level 3.

- **Level 0** - the strategy is not implemented
- **Level 3** - the strategy is fully implemented

Organisations aim to improve their maturity level over time.

# What is the ISM?

::content::

The **Information Security Manual (ISM)** is a much larger framework.

It is published by the **Australian Signals Directorate (ASD)**.

It gives detailed guidance for protecting systems and data from cyber threats.

It covers four areas: **govern, protect, detect,** and **respond**.

> "Govern" relates to the policies and processes an organisation uses to manage cyber security. It includes risk management, security governance, and compliance with laws and regulations.

# ISM in Practice

::left::

### The ISM helps organisations

- Assess their cyber security risk
- Apply consistent, tested controls
- Follow government-endorsed guidance

::right::

### Developers should

- Follow the secure coding controls in the ISM
- Apply the guidelines that suit their system
- Use it alongside the Essential Eight

# How They Relate

::content::

The **Essential Eight** is small and specific - eight clear and measurable strategies.

The **ISM** is broad - it covers many more controls in detail.

The Essential Eight strategies are included **inside** the ISM.

Together, they guide how organisations protect their systems and identify what the government expects from organisations in protecting privacy.

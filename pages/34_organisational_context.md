# Goals

::content::

Goals are **broad statements** that describe what an organisation wants to achieve.

They are usually **long-term** and provide a sense of direction for the organisation.

Goals are often qualitative and may not be easily measurable.

**Examples:**

- To increase customer satisfaction by providing high-quality products and services.
- To expand the company's market share by entering new markets and increasing sales.
- To improve employee engagement and retention by creating a positive work environment.

# Objectives

::content::

Objectives are more **specific and measurable** statements that describe **how** an organisation will achieve its goals.

Objective should include a clear target and timeframe for achievement.

**Examples:**

- To increase customer satisfaction by 10% within the next 12 months by implementing a new customer feedback system.
- To expand the company's market share by 5% within the next 2 years by launching a new product line and increasing marketing efforts.
- To improve employee engagement and retention by 15% within the next 6 months by implementing a new employee recognition program and providing professional development opportunities.

# Why do we need to understand goals and objectives for Software Development?

::content::

- Any software solution needs to align with the goals and objectives of the organisation it is being developed for.
- Goals and objectives provide a clear direction for the software development process, ensuring that the solution meets the needs of the organisation and its stakeholders.
- Evaluating the success of a software solution requires measuring its impact on the organisation's goals and objectives, which can help identify areas for improvement and inform future development efforts.

# In-house vs External Software Development

::content::

- **In-house development** refers to software that is developed by the organisation's own employees or team
- **External development** refers to software that is developed by an external vendor or contractor.
- In both scenarios, development can occur as a self-contained project, ongoing development, or as part of a larger project.

::title::

### In-house development

 Software is developed by the organisation's own employees or team.

::left::

**Advantages:**

- The organisation has full control over the development process and can tailor the solution to its specific needs.
- Knowledge and expertise of the solution is retained for future maintenance and updates.
- The organisation can respond quickly to changes in requirements or issues that arise during development.

::right::

**Disadvantages:**

- The organisation needs to have the necessary skills and resources to develop the software
- The development process may take longer and be more expensive than using an external vendor.
- Developing internally may result in less clarity in the requirements stage, as the lack of a contract reduces the need for a clear specification of requirements.

---

::title::

### External development

 Software is developed by an external vendor or contractor.

::left::

**Advantages:**

- The organisation can leverage the expertise and experience of the external vendor, which may result in a higher quality solution.
- The development process may be faster and more cost-effective than developing in-house.
- Security may be improved as the external vendor may have more experience in implementing security measures and protocols.

::right::

**Disadvantages:**

- Less control over the development process.  They may not be able to tailor the solution to any changing needs.
- Knowledge and expertise of the solution may be lost at the end of the project.
- The organisation may be dependent on the external vendor for ongoing support and maintenance
- An external vendor needs to make a profit from the project, which may result in a higher cost for the organisation.
